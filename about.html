What this app is This app helps people get emergency assistance during a suspected overdose.

With one action,
Call emergency services
Alert nearby trained responders

Designed to work quickly, under stress, and with limited setup or connectivity.
What this app is not This app does not replace emergency services

This app does not guarantee help will arrive

Alerts are optional for responders

Responders are not obligated to act

Please always call emergency services whenever possible.
When to use this app: Use this app if you believe someone may be experiencing a drug overdose, including when:
Someone collapses or becomes unresponsive after using drugs
Breathing is slow, irregular, or has stopped
You cannot wake the person
You are unsure what is happening but are concerned

You do not need to be certain to use this app. For other medical emergencies, call 911 directly.
What happens when you press the button? Emergency services are called
Nearby trained responders are alerted
Your location is shared to help responders find you

Emergency services are always the priority.
Who are trained responders? Trained responders are people who have basic overdose response knowledge and have chosen to receive alerts.

They may include:
Community members
Peers
Outreach or harm reduction workers

Responders:
Always choose whether to respond
Are not guaranteed to arrive
Do not replace emergency services
About the creator This app was created by Matthew Lit as a personal project focused on the crisis of overdoses across areas such as Downtown Eastside.

The idea came from seeing how often overdoses happen in public spaces and the phenomenon known as the bystander effect.

This app is not a medical service and does not replace emergency care.
Its goal is to make it easier to alert nearby people who may have overdose response training, so help can arrive sooner.

This project is community-driven and evolving; feedback and suggestions are welcome.
