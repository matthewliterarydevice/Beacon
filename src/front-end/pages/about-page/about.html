What this app is Beacon helps close the gap between spotting a suspected overdose and a trained, naloxone-carrying responder arriving in time to help.

It runs in two modes:
Nudge Mode — for when you're not sure what you're seeing. A few short questions help decide what's happening; you don't have to be certain.
Alert Mode — for when you're ready to act. One tap calls 911, shares your location, and notifies nearby trained responders, all at once.

Designed to work quickly, under stress, and with limited setup or connectivity.
What this app is not This app does not replace 911 or emergency services — Alert Mode calls 911 first, every time, alongside notifying responders

This app does not guarantee help will arrive

This app does not diagnose or direct medical care

This app does not certify or rank responders, or take responsibility for their outcomes

Alerts are optional for responders — they can accept or ignore any alert, with no penalty or tracking
When to use this app: Use this app if you believe someone may be experiencing a drug overdose, including when:
Someone collapses or becomes unresponsive after using drugs
Breathing is slow, irregular, or has stopped
You cannot wake the person
You are unsure what is happening but are concerned

You do not need to be certain to use this app. Any unclear answer in Nudge Mode automatically escalates to Alert Mode. For other medical emergencies, call 911 directly.
What happens when you press the button? In Nudge Mode: a few short questions on your phone help figure out what's happening before anything becomes a visible alert
In Alert Mode: emergency services (911) are called
Nearby trained responders are alerted at the same time
Your location is shared so both EMS and responders can find you
Who are responders? Responders are trained, naloxone-carrying people who've opted in to receive alerts. They're onboarded only through partner organizations — there's no open, public sign-up.

They may include:
Outreach and peer workers
Healthcare workers
Staff at partner businesses who've completed partner-run naloxone training

Responders:
Always choose whether to respond
Are not guaranteed to arrive
Do not replace emergency services
Where Beacon works right now Beacon's launch is scoped to two Vancouver neighborhoods:
Downtown Eastside (DTES)
Downtown Vancouver

These areas were chosen for overdose density, responder density, and bystander foot traffic. Coverage may expand to other neighborhoods over time.
About the creator This app was created by Matthew Lit as a personal project focused on the crisis of overdoses across areas such as Downtown Eastside.

The idea came from seeing how often overdoses happen in public spaces and the phenomenon known as the bystander effect.

This app is not a medical service and does not replace emergency care.
Its goal is to make it easier to alert nearby people who may have overdose response training, so help can arrive sooner.

This project is community-driven and evolving; feedback and suggestions are welcome.
